Aim

To understand the Fourier series representation of periodic functions and compute Fourier coefficients. To analyze how any periodic signal can be decomposed into sine and cosine components, and visualize the convergence of partial sums. The virtual lab bridges theory with practical applications in vibrations, heat transfer, and signal processing.

Formula Sheet
Formula 1 — Integration by Parts
\(\displaystyle\int u\,v\,dx = u\,v_1 - u'v_2 + u''v_3 - u'''v_4 + \cdots\)
Dashes = differentiation; subscripts = repeated integration.
Example: \(\displaystyle\int(\pi^2-x^2)\cos nx\,dx = (\pi^2-x^2)\frac{\sin nx}{n} - (-2x)\!\left(-\frac{\cos nx}{n^2}\right) + (-2)\!\left(-\frac{\sin nx}{n^3}\right)\)
Formula 2 — Exponential × Trigonometric
\((i)\;\displaystyle\int e^{ax}\sin bx\,dx = \frac{e^{ax}}{a^2+b^2}(a\sin bx - b\cos bx)\)
\((ii)\;\displaystyle\int e^{ax}\cos bx\,dx = \frac{e^{ax}}{a^2+b^2}(a\cos bx + b\sin bx)\)
Formula 3 — Product-to-Sum
\((i)\;2\sin A\cos B = \sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\)
\((ii)\;2\cos A\sin B = \sin(A+B)-\sin(A-B)\)
\((iii)\;2\cos A\cos B = \cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)\)
\((iv)\;2\sin A\sin B = \cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)\)
Formula 4 — Sum-to-Product
\((i)\;\sin A+\sin B = 2\sin\dfrac{A+B}{2}\cos\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)
\((ii)\;\sin A-\sin B = 2\cos\dfrac{A+B}{2}\sin\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)
\((iii)\;\cos A+\cos B = 2\cos\dfrac{A+B}{2}\cos\dfrac{A-B}{2}\)
\((iv)\;\cos A-\cos B = 2\sin\dfrac{A+B}{2}\sin\dfrac{B-A}{2}\)
Formula 5 — Compound Angle
\((i)\;\sin(A\pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B\)
\((ii)\;\cos(A\pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B\)
Formula 6 — Values at Integer Multiples of π
\(\sin n\pi = 0,\quad \sin 2n\pi = 0\)
\(\cos n\pi = (-1)^n,\quad \cos 2n\pi = 1\)
For any integer n.
Formula 7 — Shifted Values
\(\sin(n\pm1)\pi = 0\)
\(\cos(n\pm1)\pi = -\cos n\pi\)
For any integer n.

Fourier Series Theory

A Fourier series is an expansion of a periodic function in terms of an infinite sum of sines and cosines. Any piecewise smooth periodic function can be expressed as a Fourier series using the orthogonality of sine and cosine functions.

General Fourier Series
General Form — period 2L
\[\displaystyle f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L} + b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right]\]
Fourier Coefficients
Integral Formulas
\[\displaystyle a_0 = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L}f(x)\,dx\] \[\displaystyle a_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L}f(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx\] \[\displaystyle b_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L}f(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx\]
Even and Odd Functions

Even — f(−x) = f(x)

Only cosine terms; all \(b_n = 0\).

\(a_0 = \dfrac{2}{L}\displaystyle\int_0^{L}f(x)\,dx\)

\(a_n = \dfrac{2}{L}\displaystyle\int_0^{L}f(x)\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx\)

Odd — f(−x) = −f(x)

Only sine terms; \(a_0=0\) and all \(a_n=0\).

\(b_n = \dfrac{2}{L}\displaystyle\int_0^{L}f(x)\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx\)

Dirichlet's Conditions
Sufficient Conditions for Convergence

1. f(x) is periodic with period 2L.
2. f(x) is single-valued and bounded on [−L, L].
3. f(x) has a finite number of maxima and minima in [−L, L].
4. f(x) has a finite number of discontinuities in [−L, L].

At a point of continuity the series converges to f(x). At a jump discontinuity, it converges to the average of the left and right limits.

Parseval's Identity
Energy Relation
\[\displaystyle \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L}[f(x)]^2\,dx = \frac{a_0^2}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2 + b_n^2)\]
